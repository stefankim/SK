How an engagement actually goes.

No discovery theatre. Four steps, and you can stop after any of them.

01

Read the system

Two days in the codebase and with the people who use it. You get a written assessment whether or not we continue.

02

Cut the scope

Most briefs contain twice the work they need. We agree what ships first and what is explicitly not happening.

03

Ship in slices

Something real goes live every two weeks — behind a flag if need be. No six-month reveal.

04

Hand it over

Docs, tests and a walkthrough with your team. The goal is that you do not need me next quarter.

04

Before a contract, people ask:

01What does an engagement cost?+

Day rate for ongoing work, fixed price for a defined scope after the assessment in step one. Ask in the first email and you get a number, not a discovery call.

02Can you work with our existing team?+

That is most of the work. I join your repo, your standups and your review process rather than running a parallel project, and I write code your team can maintain after I leave.

03Is our Drupal 7 site a lost cause?+

No. I have migrated several, including a 120k-page consolidation across four legacy CMSes. The question is usually what to leave behind, not whether it is possible.

04Do we actually need AI in this?+

Often not. Where it earns its place — document extraction, retrieval over a messy corpus — I build it with citations and permission checks so it can be audited. Where it does not, I will say so.

05What is your availability?+

Taking on new work from January, up to two concurrent clients. Short audits and second opinions can usually start sooner.
